About This Site

Updated October 1, 2026

This site publishes a small number of long PowerPoint guides instead of a large number of short ones. Each guide covers one task end to end, including the parts that go wrong, and is written against the current desktop version of PowerPoint rather than copied from another tutorial.

Who writes this

I am not affiliated with Microsoft. Nothing here is sponsored, and no software vendor pays for placement in these guides.

How these guides are written

Every page on this site is held to the same standard. If a guide does not meet it, the guide gets fixed or cut — it does not get published anyway.

  • Menu paths are checked against Microsoft's own documentation. Every guide ends with the Microsoft Support articles covering that task, so any instruction can be checked against the source. Where Microsoft documents a path, the guide uses Microsoft's own wording rather than a paraphrase — instructions say File → Info → Compress Media because that is where Microsoft says it is. Where no Microsoft article covers a step, the guide describes what the current desktop build does and leaves the citation out rather than attaching one that does not carry the claim. Menu names reflect PowerPoint 2024 and later.
  • Platform differences are stated, not glossed over. Where a shortcut differs on Mac, or a feature is missing there altogether, the guide says so — describing a menu item the reader cannot find is the single most common failure in PowerPoint tutorials.
  • The failure cases are treated as part of the task. Almost nobody searches for a procedure. They search because something did not work. Every guide therefore names the specific ways the task goes wrong and what each one means.
  • Diagrams are labelled as diagrams. The illustrations on this site are drawn to explain a concept — how a Slide Master cascades, how the three animation Start settings differ. They are not screenshots, and they are captioned so nobody mistakes them for one.
  • Dates change when the content changes. The date at the top of a guide is its last substantive edit. It is not touched to look fresh.

What this site does not do

  • No page for every keyword. If two searches are asking the same question, they get one page. Splitting a topic across near-identical pages helps nobody who is trying to learn the task.
  • No padding to hit a length. Guides are as long as the task is. A short task gets a short page.
  • No guessing at features. Anything that could not be reproduced in the current build is left out rather than described from memory.

Corrections

Software moves. If a menu path here no longer matches what you see, or a step did not work on your version, that is worth reporting — a wrong step costs someone an afternoon.

Email kaixinjin0205@gmail.com with the page, your PowerPoint version and platform, and what you saw instead. Corrections are made to the page itself, and the date at the top changes when they are.

Where to start

If you are new to PowerPoint, start with How to Use PowerPoint — it walks through building a first presentation in eight steps and then points to the guide for whatever you need next. If something has already gone wrong, recovering unsaved work and compressing a file that got too big are the two pages people arrive at most often.
